# render3d — the plan

`Render3D.asi` draws the whole 3D view itself, in Rust, in place of the engine's
`Armada_RenderAllOurStuff` (0x598110). The engine keeps the simulation, the 2D HUD,
the menus, the device, the window and `Present`. This file is the working plan. It
changes as roadblocks show up, and every change is noted under "Changes to the plan".

Background and measurements live outside the repo, in
`$A2_DATA/notes/renderer-inventory/`: `README.md` and the reports it lists.

## Decisions

- **Rust, `#![no_std]` + `alloc`.** It targets `i686-pc-windows-msvc` and links with
  `lld-link /nodefaultlib` like the C plugins, with `kernel32` as the only import. The
  crate provides its own allocator (`HeapAlloc`), `mem*`, `__chkstk` and `_fltused`,
  and uses `panic = abort` with a panic handler that logs.
- **The device is the d3d9 one behind d3d8to9** (option A in `integration.md`). The
  engine's back buffer stays the engine's, and `RefreshDisplay` stays the only
  `Present`. Without a d3d9 device, the plugin calls the stock body and the game is
  stock.
- **The seam is the body of `Armada_RenderAllOurStuff`,** for both views: main, and
  the HUD's action-camera inset, keyed by its viewport. We keep the engine's
  `GameObject_PreRenderAll`, `Ordnance::PreRenderAll` and `DisplayInterface::PreRenderAll`
  calls, because the HUD and the sim read what they compute.
- **The body is a list of steps.** Each step is either ours or the engine's own
  sub-function, which is the fallback. Every category can be switched back to stock in
  `Render3D.ini`, so the game works from the first milestone and modules replace
  steps one at a time.
- **The cosmetic simulation is ours** (the user's decision): particles, sprite-node
  animation, debris, animation channels, selection, warp, LOD, fades and the cloak
  timeline. It follows `spec-particles.md` and `spec-chunks-anim-lod.md`, faithful to
  stock including the quirks that make up the look, and runs on the sim clock
  (`Get_Time`). The game sim (`GameProcess`) is only ever read.
- **Assets are ours.** We load SODs, TGAs (mip chains spelt as stock spells them),
  `.spr` files and ODFs from the game directory. Textures go into `D3DPOOL_DEFAULT`
  through a system-memory staging copy that is released afterwards, so they cost no
  address space in a process that is not large-address-aware (2 GB).
- **Frame:** a linear-light HDR target (`A16B16G16R16F`) with MSAA and our own depth,
  per view. It is resolved, bloomed and tone-mapped into the engine's back buffer at
  the view's rectangle. Our draws then clear the engine's depth in that rectangle for
  the HUD. The tone map is near-identity up to stock's white and rolls off above it,
  so stock content looks as it did and HDR highlights have room.
- **New in this renderer** (not possible on the old one):
  1. HDR scene radiance with physically based bloom: explosions, weapons, suns and
     self-illumination above white bloom by their energy, not by an 8-bit threshold.
  2. Shadows drawn from this frame's hulls, ahead of the scene (no one-frame lag).
  3. MSAA on our own targets, so the back buffer stays 1x and lockable, and
     `MSAA.asi`'s edge fill is not needed.
- **It supersedes these plugins:** Lighting, Sky, Nebulae, Surfaces, Explosions,
  Shields, Repair, Venting, Specials, Fog, Planets and MSAA. Their looks and recipes
  are ported into modules. `render3d/install.sh` removes their `.asi` files from the
  game, and `Render3D.asi` stands down if it finds one loaded. HUD, Menus, QOL, Grid,
  Online and binkproxy are unaffected.

## Layout

    render3d/
      Cargo.toml, .cargo/config.toml, build.sh, install.sh, Render3D.ini
      kernel32.def (+ d3d9 not linked: reached through COM)
      shaders/*.hlsl -> src/shaders.rs (generated by hlsl-rs.sh, committed, stamped)
      src/
        lib.rs       DllMain, init, panic handler
        rt/          alloc, mem, chkstk, log, ini, file, math, str
        d3d/         d3d9 COM: device, resources, state, shaders, targets
        engine/      addresses, accessors, hooks, camera, objects, lights, clock
        assets/      sod, tga, spr, odf, caches
        frame/       views, steps, targets, post (bloom, tonemap), stock fallback
        mods/        sky, stars, hulls, sprites/particles, chunks, planets, asteroids,
                     nebulae, ordnance, explosions, shields, selection, fog, effects

## Milestones

1. **Toolchain and skeleton.** The crate builds, `Render3D.asi` loads, logs and
   stands down cleanly. `build.sh`, `install.sh`, the ini.
2. **Frame takeover with full stock fallback.** Our body runs every engine step into
   the HDR target, then resolve, bloom and tone map into the back buffer, with depth
   fixed for the HUD and both views handled. The game plays as stock with HDR bloom.
   Bench: `showcase`, HUD on, the inset, a whole match.
3. **Assets.** SOD, TGA, `.spr`, ODF; texture and mesh caches; checked against all 451
   SODs offline.
4. **Modules, in parallel.** Each one replaces engine steps and ports a plugin's look:
   - hulls (meshes, nodes, LOD, animation, team colour, Borg bump, Lighting's
     shading, shadows, cloak, fades, warp stretch);
   - sky, starfield and background planets (Sky);
   - sprite nodes, emitters and particles (the spec; Venting, Repair);
   - planets, moons and asteroids (Surfaces, Lighting's planets, tessellation);
   - nebulae (Nebulae, plus the stock puffs);
   - ordnance, beams, projectiles, special weapons and the warp flash (Specials);
   - explosions, shockwaves and debris (Explosions, the chunk spec);
   - shields, selection, ghosts and the build ghost, fog and grid (Shields, Fog);
   - the long tail (special-object effects, nanites, construction): stock fallback
     first, then ours.
5. **Integration.** `./install` installs render3d in place of the superseded layers;
   an `a2mod` layer; a test-bench scenario; `no-assets`; README, CHANGELOG and root
   changelog; `publish/check.sh`.
6. **Done when** a whole match plays on the bench with every category ours, and
   `./install` puts it in place with one call. The user's in-game sign-off follows,
   as always.

## Changes to the plan

- 2026-10-14: **Rust instead of C** (the user's request). Toolchain is rustup with
  `i686-pc-windows-msvc` in `~/.rustup`, and `lld-link`; spike and skeleton build with
  kernel32 as the only import. CI will need `rustup target add i686-pc-windows-msvc`.
- 2026-10-14: the body is mapped (`body-map.md` in the notes). The signature is
  `void __cdecl Armada_RenderAllOurStuff(Armada_Camera*, Render_Stuff&, bool isMain)`,
  with 13 steps; every step but the inline stores, the object loop and credits can be
  called on its own (the object loop's fallback is `GameObject_RenderAll` 0x597fb0).
  `Background_Render` also draws BackgroundObjects and owns the only colour clear. The
  particle list is drawn per view but pruned once a frame.
- 2026-10-14: the sim reads nothing the render pass writes (`sim-render-coupling.md`,
  two-player match with one side blind for 4 minutes). Rule: never draw from
  `g_random` (0x7b0fac); render randomness is ours or `g_asyncRandom`.
- 2026-10-15: **the plugins stay working while modules arrive.** The step body calls
  each engine step through its call site, so every plugin hooked there still draws,
  into our target; a module stands down while the plugin it replaces is loaded,
  instead of the whole renderer standing down. Only `MSAA.asi` is removed at once.
- 2026-10-15: a database does not keep its SOD's name (`+0x8` is not a string on
  `ST3D_Database`); the SOD is named after the object's ODF, scaled by its `ScaleSOD`.
- 2026-10-15: `GameFeature_RenderAll` is re-implemented as a loop (each feature with
  flag bit 4 at `+0x20` gets `Render(cam, isMain)`, vtable `+0x20`), so modules take
  features one by one.

## Milestone 4: how a module is built

The frame (`frame/`) runs the body as steps (`frame/steps.rs`); a module
(`mods/<name>.rs`, registered with one `add(...)` line in `mods/mod.rs`) takes over
whatever it draws through the `Module` trait:

- `step(Step::X)`: a whole engine step (sky and background, terrain, ordnance,
  specials, warp, chunks, particles, selection; `Translucent` runs just before the
  device's `Flush`);
- `object(o)`: one object of the object loop, in place of its `Render`;
- `feature(f)`: one game feature of `GameFeature_RenderAll` (explosions, shield hits,
  sparks, construction, ghosts, the build ghost...), in place of its `Render`;
- `begin_view`/`end_view`, `device`, `reset` (drop the default pool before a `Reset`).

What every module uses, rather than writing its own:

- `assets`: SODs, textures (stock mip chains), the sprite table, ODFs, by name;
- `scene`: SOD instances (`scene::model`, `pose::pose`, `scene::draw` or
  `draw_part` with one's own shaders), the view's registered lights
  (`scene::lights()`), the translucent queue;
- `gfx`: `Guard` (put the engine's device state back), `Shader`/`Decl` made on first
  use, `DynVb` for geometry rebuilt every frame, `PerView` for per-view state (the HUD's
  action camera is a second view: `View::key`);
- `engine`, `engine::obj`: addresses and reads of the engine's state. **Never write
  anything the simulation reads**, never draw from `g_random` (0x7b0fac).
- Shaders: `shaders/<name>.hlsl` with an `// entries:` first line; `build.sh` compiles
  it into `src/shaders/<name>.rs`. vkd3d unrolls loops and has no `tex3Dlod`; keep
  branches out of loops.

Rules for a module:

1. **Off until it is right.** Register it `false` (off unless `[Modules] <name>=1`)
   until it matches stock (or the plugin it ports) on the bench, then `true`.
2. **Stand down beside its plugin**: `superseded()` names the `.asi` whose work it
   replaces, so an install that still has that plugin keeps its look.
3. **Faithful to stock's look and quirks** (the user wants the early-2000s charm), and
   to the ported plugin's look where there is one: the plugins' READMEs say what was
   settled with the user and must not be re-opened.
4. **Cosmetic simulation is ours**, on the sim clock (`View::time`), following
   `spec-particles.md` and `spec-chunks-anim-lod.md` in the notes.
5. **Write each engine finding into this crate's README** beside the code that needs
   it, in words (hard rule 9): names, addresses, offsets, never listings.
6. Bench evidence for every claim, against stock or the plugin at the same moment
   (`scene pause`, then compare), main view and inset (`scene "hud on"`).

### The modules, and who builds them (2026-10-15)

| Module | Takes over | Ports | Branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| hulls | objects drawn by `GameObject::Render`: ships, stations, anomalies, scrap; their sprite nodes; fades, cloak, warp stretch, team colour, Borg bump, same-frame shadows | `Lighting.asi` (hulls) | `r3d-hulls` |
| sky | `Step::Background`: sky cube or recipe, starfield, background planets and suns | `Sky.asi` | `r3d-sky` |
| particles | `Step::Particles`, emitters on SOD nodes, nebula puffs, billboard effects: our own particle simulation | `Venting.asi` | `r3d-particles` |
| ordnance | `Step::Ordnance`, `Step::Warp`: beams, projectiles, special weapons, the warp flash | `Specials.asi` | `r3d-ordnance` |
| effects | features: explosions (fireball, shockwave, Omega), shield hits, hit sparks and repair, construction, bees; `Step::Chunks` (our own debris), `Step::Specials` | `Explosions.asi`, `Shields.asi`, `Repair.asi` | `r3d-effects` |
| world | objects that override `Render`: planets, moons, asteroid fields, nebulae (volumes), black holes and the like | `Surfaces.asi`, `Planets.asi`, `Nebulae.asi`, `Lighting.asi` (planets) | `r3d-world` |
| overlays | `Step::Terrain` (grid, fog, shroud, footprints), `Step::Selection`, ghosts, the build ghost | `Fog.asi` | `r3d-overlays` |

Each branch starts from the commit that added this section and is merged into
`worktree-renderer` by the integrator. A module touches `mods/<name>.rs` (or
`mods/<name>/`), `shaders/<name>*.hlsl`, its line in `mods/mod.rs`, and its section of
`README.md`; a change to the shared layers (`scene`, `gfx`, `assets`, `engine`,
`frame`) is kept small and named in the commit message, since others build on it.
